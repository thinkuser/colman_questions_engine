# Result Experience V1

## Goal
The result page should feel like a useful advisor conversation, not a quiz scorecard.

## Required sections

### 1. Hero
Show the best-fit program immediately, plus a short explanation.
Example: `The direction that best fits what you told us: Data Science.`
Do not show a fake percentage score.

Also show ranked qualitative labels:
- Strongest fit
- Also relevant / good fit
- Less aligned right now

### 2. Why this result
Show 3–5 candidate answers that materially drove the result.
Example:
- preferred prediction/modeling over app-building;
- wanted to learn what can be predicted from a large dataset;
- preferred statistics/ML subject group;
- math tolerance was adequate/high.

### 3. Mirror
Reflect a concise candidate statement and ask whether it feels accurate.
Example: `Give me lots of information and an unsolved problem; I want to find the pattern and predict what happens next.`
Actions:
- `Yes, that sounds like me`
- `Not exactly — refine`
The mirror can create an additional feedback signal but must not silently rewrite historical answers.

### 4. Main Decision / trade-off
Explain why #1 beat #2 using one clear axis.
Examples:
- CS vs DS: build the technology ↔ learn/predict from data.
- CS vs MIS: technology itself ↔ technology in a business/organizational context.
- DS vs MIS: data/modeling as the profession ↔ data as a tool for organizational decisions.

### 5. What you'll learn
Translate curriculum into 3–5 understandable learning worlds, not a raw course dump.
For Data Science example:
- mathematical foundations;
- coding/computing;
- ML/AI/modeling;
- practical data problem solving.

### 6. What you'll do
Use a real-world challenge story and a simple workflow.
Example: `We have one million customers. Who is likely to churn?`
Then show: collect → clean/understand → find patterns → model → test → explain → act.

### 7. Career paths
Show a small, credible career map based on official program information. Avoid implying guaranteed employment.

### 8. Reality Check
Surface important trade-offs honestly.
Example for Data Science: AI/ML are important, but the degree also requires substantial mathematics, statistics, and programming.

### 9. Secondary program
Do not hide #2. Explain why it may still fit and the deciding distinction.
Offer `Compare again`.

### 10. Why COLMAN
Only after the fit explanation. Connect institutional benefits to candidate concerns, e.g. practical experience, career development, industry exposure, current skills/AI.

### 11. CTAs
Recommended hierarchy:
1. Check admission requirements.
2. Compare again with the secondary program.
3. Speak with an advisor about this specific decision.

Contextual advisor CTA example:
`I want to speak with an advisor about Data Science vs Computer Science.`

## Special states
### Near tie
Do not force a winner. Say the candidate is genuinely between two programs and present the decisive axis. Optionally ask one extra tie-break question.

### No strong fit
Explain that the selected programs may not match the candidate's preferences well and suggest exploring other program clusters when available.

## Core output philosophy
The result must answer not only `What won?`, but `What is the decision I actually need to make?`
